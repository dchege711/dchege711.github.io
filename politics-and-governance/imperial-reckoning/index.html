Pax Britannica

“Pax Britannica” is Latin for “British Peace”. It’s a period of relative peace between the great powers between 1815 (end of the Napoleonic Wars) and 1914 (First World War).

The messaging to Britons. We need the Uganda railway to spread Christianity, destroy slave trade, and protect the Nile. If Germany dams the Nile, then Egypt dries up, losing us the Suez Canal, and therefore control of India.

Building the railway takes ~30K “coolies” from India. ~30% die from working conditions, disease, and maneating lions. Africans living close to the railway adversely affected by British brutality, and diseases like smallpox.

How to make Kenya profitable to pay back railroad construction? Pitch cheap land, abundant labor, and large potential profits to Britons. That said, Kenya was never capable of paying its railway debt and the British government finally wrote it off in the 1930s.

Prior to the 1890s, Britan had informal colonies that were chained to Britain via imperialist free trade. Formal colonies like India and Hong Kong were rare. Economic depression in Britain, and the industrialization of others (e.g., US, Germany) prompted formal colonies.

The White Man’s Burden. With their superior race, Christian values, and economic know-how, the British had a moral obligation to redeem the backward heathens of the world. The British were trustees for the hapless natives who had not yet evolved to a point of making responsible decisions on their own.

Trust the man on the spot. The empire was never to be a burden on British taxpayers. Every colony was expected to be self-financing. The Colonial Office in London had less than 50 people. The Kenya Administration formed the boots on the ground, e.g., provincial commissioners, district commissioners, etc. The Colonial Governor balanced between often conflicting demands of the Administration in Kenya and the Colonial Office in London.

British settlers were aristocrats looking to recreate the seigniorial lifestyle that was increasingly difficult to maintain in Britain. Lord Delamere got 100K acres in 1903, and 60K acres a few years later. Hedonism in Kenya was common, e.g., “are you married or do you live in Kenya” being a running joke.

Settlers used political ties back in London to advance their interests, e.g., low-interest loans, reduced freight charges, crop subsidies, extending land leases from 99y to 999y, getting seats in Kenya’s legislative council, etc.

Racist ideology grew. Africans were biologically inferior, had smaller brain sizes, had limited capacity for pain and emotion, had different nutritional needs, were lazy and barbaric.

Land was central to Kikuyu culture. A man needed land to accumulate resources for dowry. A woman needed land to grow crops and sustain her family. However, the Kikuyu were boxed in: settler farms to the east, south, and north; Aberdares forest reserves to the west; Nairobi to the southwest.

After WWII, the empire was the logical place to resettle demobilized British soldiers. Settlers now consisted of well-capitalized aristocrats, poor White immigrants from South Africa, and former British soldiers.

The settlers weren’t producing enough to sustain the White Man’s Enclave. Cheap labor was a lever to pull, and usually, the labor came from the Kikuyu population. Force Africans into reserves; institute taxes worth two months of wages; enforce a pass (kipande) system to limit movement outside the reserves. I was no longer a shepherd, but one of the flock, going to work on the white man’s farm with my mbugi around my neck.

Cash-strapped settler farms used African squatters. Labor for the settler for 4 months in the year in exchange for a plot for cultivation, grazing, and raising children. After 1918, the squatter plots were shrunk, the number of animals restricted, and the required labor on the settler farms increased. Note that the squatters had no land claims in the reserves.

Britain relied on indirect rule: African chiefs beneath the district administrators, but above other Africans. The Kikuyu governance system had a council of elders, and so the chief title was bogus. That said, the chiefs got large and fertile parcels in the reserves, superior seeds, internal trade licenses, and access to cheap labor.

The Kikuyu Central Association (KCA) comprised of formally-educated Africans trying to better themselves in the system, despite not being appointed African chiefs. Female circumcision became a hot topic and rallying call for the Africans. Missionaries condemned it, while KCA fought for it in the name of culture.

Post WWII, British veterans got demobilization support, e.g., land, low-interest loans, etc., while African veterans were sidelined. The Africans were aware of nationalist movements in India. WWII had been framed as a fight for the principles of self-determination against the forces of facism.

The birthplace of oath-taking was Olenguruone, where evicted Kikuyu squatters were resettled. The oath was to collectively fight the injustices of British rule. More militant members of the Kenya African Union (KAU) oathed Africans into deeper commitment to violent action. In comparison, Kenyatta and his compatriots were moderates.
